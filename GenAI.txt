Question : 
A company sells electronic products. It has many product manuals and policy documents. 
The company wants to build a support assistant where customers can ask questions like: 
"What should I do if my device shows an error code?" "Is this repair covered under warranty?" 
"How many days will delivery take?" 
Write a simple explanation of how a RAG-based support assistant should work for this company. 
What to include in your answer Write your answer in simple English and cover all the points below: 
What is the document corpus in this example? 
Why should the documents be split into chunks? 
Why are embeddings and a vector database needed? 
How does retrieval help before the LLM writes the answer? 
What should the assistant do if the answer is not found in the documents? 
Why is groundedness important for this support assistant? Mention any two possible failure points in this RAG system. 
Explain how the company can check answer quality using groundedness and relevance.


Answer: 
A RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) support assistant helps customers by answering their questions using the company's own documents instead of depending only on the LLM's general knowledge.
The document corpus is the collection of all the company's support-related documents, such as product manuals, warranty policies, repair guides, delivery information, and FAQs. These documents contain the trusted information that the assistant should use when answering customer questions.
Instead of searching through entire documents, the documents are split into smaller chunks. This makes it easier to find the exact section that matches a customer's question and improves both the speed and accuracy of the system.
Each document chunk is converted into an embedding, which is a numerical representation of its meaning. These embeddings are stored in a vector database. When a customer asks a question, the query is also converted into an embedding, and the vector database searches for the chunks that are most similar in meaning.
Before the LLM generates a response, the system first **retrieves the most relevant document chunks**. These chunks are given to the LLM as context so it can answer based on the company's actual policies and manuals instead of making assumptions or generating incorrect information.
If the system cannot find the required information in the documents, it should honestly tell the customer that it couldn't find an answer and recommend contacting customer support rather than providing an uncertain response.
Groundedness is very important because it ensures that every answer is supported by the company's documents. This reduces incorrect or made-up answers and helps customers trust the information they receive.
Two common failure points are retrieving irrelevant document chunks and having outdated or incomplete documents in the knowledge base.
The company can measure answer quality by checking groundedness and relevance. Groundedness verifies that the answer is supported by the retrieved documents, while relevance checks whether the retrieved documents are actually related to the customer's question. If both are strong, the assistant is likely to provide accurate and helpful answers.
